It protects the existing base. The initiative adds explanation and a clearer question to a journey that already converts 46.0% of starters. If the new steps push people out, this is where it shows first.
It is fast. It’s visible within the session, unlike the 60-day missed-payment rate.
It is the natural counterweight to the primary metric. Continuation at the income step could rise because people click through, and the agreement rate would catch that if they later abandon. Together they show whether more people reach the end, not just the next step.
It is what you’re already tracking. Your strategic map listed the “extra step vs. drop-off” tension, and this is the metric that tension is about.
Suggested floor (my calculation, illustrative)

A drop of up to about one point in the pilot segment is roughly break-even on kept agreements, if half the failure gap closes:

Scenario	Agreement rate	Missed payment	Kept agreements per month (2,862 starters)
Today	41.6%	22.5%	about 923
One-point drop, half the gap closed	40.6%	about 19.95%	about 930

So I’d set the floor at about 40.6% in the pilot segment and 46.0% for everyone else, and stop or review if either is breached for two consecutive weeks. The tolerance and the two-week rule are my assumptions, not figures from the pack.

The tension to decide up front

A lower agreement rate isn’t automatically bad, because some people may decide not to take a plan they couldn’t keep. The guardrail only holds if the team agrees the floor in advance, so a dip is judged against kept agreements rather than argued after the fact. Your cold-read already says success should be judged on kept plans.
