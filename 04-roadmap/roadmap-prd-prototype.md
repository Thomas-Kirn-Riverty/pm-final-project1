- **Guardrail:** Agreement rate: the share of journey starters who end in an agreement.  Baseline: 46.0% overall (3,846 of 8,360). Pilot segment baseline: 41.6% for variable-income and shift-work starters (2,862). Source: data-pack.md, Baseline rates and User split. What it is for  It is the metric that must not drop. The initiative adds explanation and a clearer question to a journey that already converts 46.0% of starters. If the new steps push people out, this is where it shows first, and it counterbalances the primary metric. Continuation at the income step could rise because people click through, and the agreement rate would catch it if they later abandon.  Suggested floor (my assumption, illustrative) Pilot segment: about 40.6%, one point below its baseline. Everyone else: 46.0%. Rule: Stop or review if either is breached for two consecutive weeks.  The one-point tolerance is roughly break-even on kept agreements if half the failure gap closes. The two-week rule is also my assumption, not a figure from the pack.  Companion checks Kept agreements per month: About 3,115. It is the final test but lags by 60 days. Fixed-salary agreement rate: 48.1%, which the pilot shouldn’t touch. The team should agree the floor in advance, so a dip is judged against kept agreements rather than argued after the fact.  Limits Normal variation: The agreement rate moved between 43.1% and 46.3% over six months, so small dips fall within normal swings. ## Roadmap
### NOW, this sprint
- **B1 Plain explainer for why income is asked**, It answers T-206, N-06 and the 419 “why income is asked” contacts (16.2%), but it won’t resolve “this month or a normal month” on its own.

### NEXT, the following sprints
- **B2 Affordability check before the plan options**, It attacks the plan-by-feel failure (N-02) and the income ambiguity (N-01) for variable-income users, but it must reuse the existing affordability answers or it risks the 19.3% step loss and the agreement guardrail.

### LATER, backlog
- **B3 Photo upload that works on mobile, or skip the document**, It fixes T-201 (853 journeys, 10.2% of starters) and protects the agreement guardrail, but it acts after the step the primary metric measures and isn’t Ana’s core friction.
- **B8 Show the shortest plan first to raise recovery**, It extends the Month 4 change, when missed payments rose from 15.6% to 18.4%, and nudges people toward the shortest-plan shortcut that failed N-02.

### ✂ Cut List
- **B4 Pick a payment date that matches pay day**, It suits shift workers, but no interview or ticket links pay day to failure, and it depends on B3 collecting the income pattern first.
- **B5 Handoff summary for the agent**, It addresses N-08, N-09 and T-205, but it doesn’t touch Ana’s friction or the primary metric.
- **B6 One plan for several open claims**, The pain is real (448 contacts, 37.7% and 27.6% agreement), but it is a separate initiative that won’t fit in 12 weeks and doesn’t move the primary metric.
- **B7 Chat assistant on every portal page	**, Chat users have a 21.6% missed-payment rate against 18.3% for non-users, though self-selection is possible, and T-208 and N-05 show chat confirming plans without consequences.
