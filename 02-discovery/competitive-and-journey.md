- **Your journey map file name (e.g. journey-map.html):** The 4 Stages (Action -> Benefit)

1. Orient

User action: Reads why income is asked and kept -> knows what she agrees to.
Internal state: Wary and short on time -> reassured, continues instead of closing the tab.
Pain point addressed: No help text, fear of data use -> trust before data entry (T-206, N-06).

2. Describe

User action: Picks “typical month” and an income range -> answers without fear of a wrong number.
Internal state: Unsure what to enter -> in control, with skip option and saved progress.
Pain point addressed: Ambiguous income question, answers lost on back button -> no restart (N-01, T-204).

3. Choose

User action: Compares only plans that fit her pay day -> stops choosing by fewest months.
Internal state: Anxious about cost -> confident, having seen a missed-payment outcome beforehand.
Pain point addressed: Plan chosen by length, no consequences shown -> informed choice (N-02, N-05, T-208).

4. Commit

User action: Confirms plan, receives reminder before due date -> first payment lands on time.
Internal state: Settled but watchful -> supported; an agent can see her entries if she calls.
Pain point addressed: Late reminders, agents blind to entries -> no repeating herself (T-207, T-205, N-08).
3 Competitive Advantages over the Manual Workaround
Fitted answer: Replaces payslip-and-calculator maths with ranged answers -> fitted plan in minutes, not a closed tab.
Consequences first: Shows missed-payment consequences before confirming -> she learns the cost before, not after, a broken plan.
Continuity: Keeps her entries and progress -> no restarting online or repeating herself on the phone.
Cautions
Internal states are design intentions. The notes record what Ana’s equivalents felt at the start (unsure, scared, not sure what happens if late), not how the new journey would feel. They need testing.
Claims are out of scope. Ana has two open claims, but one plan across several claims (B1, T-202) is a separate feature. Stage 3 plans her situation as it is today and doesn’t solve N-03’s question.
The reminder fix is a dependency. T-207 happens “in some cases”, and the file doesn’t say how often. Stage 4 only works if it is fixed.
Trade-off in Stage 2: Ranges and a skip option build trust (N-06) but reduce accuracy. This is the “data ask vs. trust” tension from your strategic map.
Not yet tested against a cheaper fix. Reversing the 6-month-first plan order might recover part of the Stage 3 gain.
